Flowers have long held a unique place in human culture, language, and emotion. From ancient times to the modern era, their significance has evolved while remaining deeply rooted in human expression. As early as 3000 BCE, in civilizations such as ancient Egypt, flowers were used in rituals, decorations, and symbolic art. Later, during the 17th century, especially between 1634–1637, the period known as Tulip Mania highlighted not only the economic value of flowers but also their cultural importance.

Flowers are more than just biological structures of plants; they act as symbols, carrying meanings that vary across cultures and contexts. In literature from the 18th and 19th centuries, particularly during the Victorian era (1837–1901), a practice known as the “language of flowers” allowed people to communicate feelings through floral arrangements. For example, a red rose symbolized love and passion, while a white lily represented purity or remembrance.

From a linguistic perspective, descriptions of flowers often involve vivid imagery and metaphorical expressions. By March 2020, with the rise of modern NLP research, flower-related datasets began being used in sentiment analysis and text classification tasks. Words like “bloom,” “petal,” and “wither” can carry both literal and symbolic meanings, which makes them useful for studying ambiguity in language models.

In more recent times, especially since January 2023, digital communication has expanded the use of floral imagery through emojis 🌸🌹 and online platforms. Social media posts during events such as December 25, 2023 often include flowers as symbols of celebration, emotion, and aesthetics.

Additionally, flowers appear in many idiomatic expressions that have developed over time. Some phrases like “pushing up daisies” date back to the mid-1800s, while others like “come up roses” became popular in the early 1900s. These expressions show how floral language evolves across generations.

In conclusion, from ancient civilizations (3000 BCE) to modern NLP developments (March 2020, January 2023), flowers remain a rich source of linguistic and cultural meaning. Their presence in both literal and symbolic forms makes them valuable for analyzing how human communication evolves over time.
